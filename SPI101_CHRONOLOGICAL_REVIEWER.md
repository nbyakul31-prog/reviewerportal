## Checkpoint 1: Week 2 — Common Ethical Theories

### 1. Introduction to Ethics
- **Definition:** Ethics broadly describes the way we look at and understand life in terms of **good or bad, right or wrong**.
- **Moral Principles:** A branch of knowledge providing frameworks when asking:
  - *"What should I do in this situation?"*
  - *"What is right or wrong for me?"*

### 2. Relativism (No Universal Moral Norms)
Relativism claims there are **no universal moral truths** binding all people.
- **2.1 Subjective Relativism:**
  - Each individual person determines morality for themselves.
  - Famous expression: *"What’s right for you may not be right for me."*
  - *Critique:* Eliminates moral accountability; serial killers or scammers could claim their acts are "right for them."
- **2.2 Cultural Relativism:**
  - Meaning of "right" and "wrong" rests strictly on **society's actual moral customs**.
  - Guidelines vary widely from place to place and time to time.
  - *Critique:* If society approves of discrimination or human rights violations, Cultural Relativism cannot condemn it.

### 3. Divine Command Theory
- Good actions = aligned with the will of God.
- Bad actions = contrary to the will of God.
- Holy Books serve as decision guides (e.g. "Honor your father and mother").
- **Crucial Characteristic for Exams:** Divine Command Theory is founded on **OBEDIENCE, NOT REASON**.

### 4. Ethical Egoism
- **Core Philosophy:** Every person should focus **exclusively on their own self-interest**.
- The morally right action is whatever provides the individual with the **maximum long-term personal benefit**.
- *Altruism note:* Helping others is allowed, but only if it serves the actor's own long-term interests (e.g., networking, building reputation).
- **Counter-Examples (Acts That Defy Egoism):**
  1. A mother depriving herself of the only bread so her starving child can eat.
  2. A father constantly working overtime solely to sustain his family.
  3. A sibling volunteering to drop out of school so other siblings can continue their education.

### 5. Consequentialism & Utilitarianism
- **Catchphrase:** *"The end will justify the means."*
- Morality is determined purely by the **outcome/consequence** of the action.
- **Utilitarianism (Jeremy Bentham):**
  - The dominant consequentialist theory.
  - Rule: *"The greatest happiness for the greatest number."*
  - The higher the number of beneficiaries, the greater the moral worth.

### 6. Kantianism / Deontology (Duty & Pure Reason)
- Author: **Immanuel Kant** (18th Century).
- Obligation-based morality that emphasizes the **nature of the action itself**, NOT its consequences.
- Moral decisions are based on **duty** and the **rights of others**.
- Morality is governed by **pure reason**—rational beings must act morally regardless of emotional impulses or personal desires.
- Maxim: *"Act morally regardless of the consequences."*

### 7. Philippine Case Study: Congress & Kantian Theory
- **1987 Constitution Article II/III Section 26:** The State guarantees equal access to public service and **prohibits political dynasties as may be defined by law**.
- **Non-Self-Executing Provision:** This section cannot be enforced without an enabling law enacted by Congress.
- **The Failure:** For over 28 years (from 1987 to 2015+), lawmakers failed to pass an anti-dynasty law because doing so destroys their political dynasties. Lawmakers prioritized self-interest (Ethical Egoism) over their constitutional duty (Kantianism).

### 8. Persuasive Power of Ethics vs The Law
- **Power Reality:** The persuasive power of ethics/morals is **weak** compared to the coercive power of the law.
- **Siesta Scenario:** 9 out of 10 people will ignore a bleeding woman knocking on their door during a siesta. But if the Bureau of Internal Revenue (BIR) sends a letter threatening criminal prosecution and imprisonment for unpaid taxes, people pay immediately!
- **Commercial Contracts:** In a ₱1,000,000 loan, lender demands a ₱1,100,000 post-dated check because legal liability compels repayment, whereas moral conscience cannot guarantee it.
- **Civil vs Natural Obligation:**
  - *Civil Obligation:* Legal duty giving a right of action in court (e.g. parents must support minor children; children can sue a non-supporting parent).
  - *Natural Obligation:* Based on equity and natural law (e.g. adult children supporting elderly parents is natural, but parents currently have no legal right of action under PH law to compel children, though bills are pending).
- **The 2000 "LOVE BUG" Virus:** Maliciously authored in the Philippines. The creator knew it was unethical, but in May 2000 no cyber law existed to penalize it. After the enactment of the **Electronic Commerce Act (RA 8792)**, copycats stopped due to fear of imprisonment.

## Checkpoint 2: Week 3–4 — Computer Ethics & Professional Codes

### 1. What is Computer Ethics?
- Defined as a new branch of ethics that grows and adapts alongside computing technology.
- Reference text: Dr. Charlemagne G. Lavina (*Social, Ethical, Legal and Professional Issues in Computing*).

### 2. James Moor's 3 Properties of Computers
James Moor argues computer ethics is unique because computers possess 3 distinct properties:
1. **Logical Malleability:** Computers can be shaped and molded to perform any activity characterized by inputs, outputs, and connecting logical operations.
2. **Impact on Society:** The pervasive presence of computers in every aspect of life.
3. **The Invisibility Factor:** Most operations are hidden from human sight. Moor identifies 3 kinds of invisibility:
   - **Invisible Abuse:** Secret unauthorized actions (e.g. logic bombs, data skimming, secret backdoors).
   - **Invisible Programming Values:** Subjective biases, shortcuts, and moral assumptions silently baked into code by programmers.
   - **Invisible Complex Calculation:** Algorithms so intricate that human verification is practically impossible.

### 3. Three Levels of Computer Ethics
- **Pop Level:** Sensitizing the general public via TV news, magazines, newspapers, and viral social media.
- **Para Level:** Collecting case studies, analyzing similarities and differences, attending forums, and studying real-world incidents.
- **Theoretical Level:** Applying classical philosophical theories (Kant, Bentham, Aristotle) to computer science problems.

### 4. Professional Codes of Ethics & 4 Benefits
A set of guidelines setting acceptable conduct for members of a profession.
1. **Ethical Decision Making**
2. **High Standards of Practice and Ethical Behavior**
3. **Trust and Respect from the General Public**
4. **Evaluation Benchmark**

### 5. Code of Ethics of Filipino IT Professionals (10 Canons)
1. Promote public knowledge, understanding, and appreciation of IT.
2. Consider general welfare and public good in all work.
3. Advertise goods and services in a clear and truthful manner.
4. Comply and strictly abide by intellectual property (IP) and patent laws.
5. Accept full responsibility for work and utilize skills with competence.
6. Make truthful statements regarding competence and product qualities.
7. Never disclose confidential client data without consent, unless mandated by law.
8. Strive to attain the highest quality in products and services.
9. Knowingly participate in the positive development of IT.
10. Continuously uphold and enhance IT standards through ongoing professional education.

### 6. AITP & ACM/IEEE Principles
- **AITP 6 Obligations:** Management, Fellow Members, Society, College/University, Employer, Country.
- **Software Engineering 8 Principles (ACM/IEEE-CS):** Public, Client & Employer, Product, Judgment, Management, Profession, Colleagues, Self.

### 7. Ten Commandments of Computer Ethics (Computer Ethics Institute)
1. Thou shalt not use a computer to harm other people.
2. Thou shalt not interfere with other people's computer work.
3. Thou shalt not snoop around in other people's computer files.
4. Thou shalt not use a computer to steal.
5. Thou shalt not use a computer to bear false witness.
6. Thou shalt not copy or use proprietary software for which you have not paid.
7. Thou shalt not use other people's computer resources without authorization.
8. Thou shalt not appropriate other people's intellectual output.
9. Thou shalt think about the social consequences of your software system.
10. Thou shalt always use a computer in ways that ensure consideration and respect.

### 8. The Hacking Community's Constitution (14 Points Summary)
- Serves as a "Bill of Rights" to be evaluated alongside the Ten Commandments.
- Highlights: Freedom of speech in cyberspace; freedom from oppressive government censorship; cyberspace democracy; hacking as a tool to test network integrity; punishment of undemocratic regimes; cyberspace as an independent governing body.
- **3 Primary Goals of Hacking/Cracking/Phreaking:**
  1. Direct democracy in cyberspace.
  2. Information should be free to all.
  3. Testing and knowing vulnerabilities in systems holding user data.
- **Open Source Stance:** Full rejection of government-mandated commercial proprietary software.

### 9. Social Networking & Philippine Context
- **Social Media Capital of the World:** Filipinos spend **53 hours/week** on social media (global average is **42 hours/week**—a gap of 11 hours!).
- **Photo Upload Rules:**
  - Netizens should not post pictures that are offensive to people in the photo.
  - Participating in a selfie/photo shoot gives implied consent to upload.
  - Taking photos/videos of private individuals without consent is generally improper.
- **3 Exemptions Where Unauthorized Photos/Videos Are Lawful:**
  1. **News of the Day:** Press information (e.g. video of a man jumping off an EDSA bridge).
  2. **General Public Welfare:** Exposing abuse (e.g. viral video of an abusive traffic enforcer threatening a truck driver, leading to immediate suspension by Manila Vice Mayor Isko Moreno).
  3. **Public Personalities in Public Places:** Photos of politicians/celebrities (e.g. Manny Pacquiao in public; NEVER in a private restroom/comfort room).

## Checkpoint 3: Week 5–6 — Technologies' Impact on Privacy & Due Process

### 1. The Right to Privacy in the 1987 Philippine Constitution
- The word **"PRIVACY"** is mentioned only **ONCE** in the 1987 Constitution, in **Article III, Section 3**:
  > *(1) The privacy of communication and correspondence shall be inviolable except upon lawful order of the court, or when public safety or order requires otherwise as prescribed by law.*  
  > *(2) Any evidence obtained in violation of this or the preceding section shall be inadmissible for any purpose in any proceeding.*
- **The Exclusionary Rule:** Evidence obtained by violating constitutional privacy is inadmissible in court ("Fruit of the poisonous tree").
- **Crucial Rule:** The right to privacy is **NOT absolute**.

### 2. The Three Lawful Exceptions to Privacy
1. **Lawful Court Order (Search Warrant):**
   - Issued under Article III, Section 2.
   - Requirements: Issued by a judge personally upon finding **probable cause** under oath or affirmation of complainant and witnesses, **particularly describing** the place to be searched and the persons or things to be seized.
2. **Public Order or Safety Requirement:**
   - E.g., LRT/MRT and mall security checks. Pedro carrying a sealed wedding gift cannot refuse inspection because public safety against terrorism overrides personal privacy.
3. **Express Provision of the Law:**
   - E.g., **Anti-Money Laundering Act (AMLA)**: Any single bank deposit of **₱300,000 or more** triggers mandatory disclosure of personal details to the Anti-Money Laundering Council (AMLC).

### 3. The Rational Relationship Test (Supreme Court Doctrine)
- Step 1: Does the law have a **reasonable purpose / rational basis**? (If yes, it is "legitimate").
- Step 2: Is the policy **reasonably related** to attaining that legitimate end? (If yes, policy is upheld).
- **Landmark Case:** Republic Act No. 3019 (**Anti-Graft and Corrupt Practices Act**). Requiring public officials to file an annual **SALN** (Statement of Assets, Liabilities, and Net Worth) does NOT violate privacy because minimizing official corruption and maintaining honesty in public service is a legitimate state interest.

### 4. Reasonable Expectation of Privacy
- Illustrated by former President Joseph "Erap" Estrada inside a voting booth during the 2010 presidential elections.
- **Anti-Wiretapping Act (RA 4200):**
  - Requires the consent of **ALL parties** to a private conversation.
  - The Supreme Court cannot rewrite the law to allow one-party consent because the wisdom of Congress on political questions must be respected.
- **Privacy Between Spouses:**
  - The Supreme Court ruled: Marital intimacy does **NOT** give either spouse the right to break open locked drawers, cabinets, or phones to search for proof of adultery/infidelity.
  - Spouses retain individual constitutional rights to privacy.
  - Marital communications are legally privileged.
- **Privacy Rights of Convicted / Detained Persons:**
  - Under RA 7438, detainees and prisoners have a **diminished expectation of privacy** inherent to lawful imprisonment and facility security.

### 5. Data Privacy Act of 2012 (RA 10173)
- Regulates the processing of personal information in both government and private sectors.
- Declares that individuals have the right to control how their personal data is collected, used, and disclosed.
- **Section 11 Core Principles:**
  - **(a) Specified and Legitimate Purpose:** Clearly stated before collection.
  - **(b) Processed Fairly and Lawfully.**
  - **(c) Accurate, Relevant, and Up-to-Date:** Inaccurate data must be rectified or deleted.
  - **(d) Adequate and Not Excessive:** Data minimization.
  - **(e) Retained Only as Long as Necessary:** Kept only while the declared purpose subsists.
  - **(f) Identification Format Limit:** Kept in identifiable format only for the required duration.
- The **Personal Information Controller (PIC)** bears legal responsibility.

### 6. Due Process Clause & Software Engineering
- **Article III Section 1:** *"No person shall be deprived of life, liberty, or property without due process of law, nor shall any person be denied the equal protection of the laws."*
- **Origin:** Started conceptually in the Garden of Eden, where God gave Adam an opportunity to explain before imposing judgment.
- **Judicial Definition:** *"A law which hears before it condemns; which proceeds upon inquiry, and renders judgment only after trial."*
- **Application in Software Engineering:**
  - When designing software, developers implement due process by granting the user the **right to be heard** whenever there is a clear danger of data destruction.
  - Example: When closing an unsaved document in Microsoft Word (`complete.doc`), the system intercepts with a prompt:  
    `"Do you want to save the changes to complete.doc? [Yes] [No] [Cancel]"`  
  - This dialog embodies due process in computing: hearing the user's intent before executing destructive action.
