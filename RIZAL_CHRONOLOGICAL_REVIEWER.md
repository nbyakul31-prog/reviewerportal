## Checkpoint 1: Republic Act No. 1425 (The Rizal Law) & The Selection of the National Hero

### 1. Historical Background & Post-WWII Context
- **Post-WWII Philippine Conditions (1950s):** Following World War II and the formal grant of independence in 1946, the Philippines continued to suffer severe economic, political, and military domination by the United States.
- **The Impetus for Nationalism:** Prominent nationalist leaders sought an intellectual and spiritual re-awakening among Filipino youth, who were increasingly gravitating toward foreign culture, products, and colonial mentality.
- **Key Proponents:**
  - **Senator Claro M. Recto:** The brilliant nationalist intellect and original author of the bill. He served as the chief opposition to the pro-American policies of Presidents Elpidio Quirino and Ramon Magsaysay.
  - **Senator Jose P. Laurel:** Chairman of the Senate Committee on Education who formally sponsored and defended **Senate Bill No. 438** on the Senate floor on **April 17, 1956**.

### 2. The Legislative Battle & Fierce Controversy
- **Original Bill (Senate Bill 438):**
  - Title: *"An Act to make Noli Me Tangere and El Filibusterismo compulsory reading matter in all public and private colleges and universities and for other purposes."*
  - Mandated the **compulsory reading** of the unexpurgated editions of *Noli Me Tangere* and *El Filibusterismo*.
- **The Catholic Church & Conservative Opposition:**
  - Led on the floor by Catholic Senators: **Sen. Decoroso Rosales**, **Sen. Mariano Cuenco**, and **Sen. Francisco Rodrigo**.
  - Strongly backed by the **Catholic Action of Manila (CAM)**, which converted its official organ, the ***Sentinel***, from a weekly publication into a daily attack paper.
  - **Core Arguments of the Opposition:**
    1. *Freedom of Speech & Religion:* Sen. Rodrigo, Rosales, and Cuenco argued that forcing students to read materials contrary to their religious faith violated constitutional liberties (citing US Supreme Court flag-salute rulings).
    2. *Allegation of Anti-Catholic Heresy:* **Fr. Jesus Cavanna** declared at a CAM symposium that out of 333 pages in the *Noli*, only **25 pages** contained patriotic passages, while **120 pages** were direct attacks against the Catholic Church.
    3. *Loss of Faith:* Sen. Francisco Rodrigo stated on the Senate floor: *"I cannot allow my son who is now 16 to read the Noli Me Tangere and the El Filibusterismo lest he lose his faith."*
    4. *Political Vendetta Accusation:* Radio commentator **Narciso Pimentel Jr.** accused Recto of introducing the bill as revenge against the Catholic electorate who, along with Magsaysay, caused Recto's poor showing in the 1955 senatorial elections.
    5. *Endangering Salvation:* Radio commentator **Jesus Paredes** warned that reading the novels' objectionable parts would endanger Catholic salvation.
- **The Church's Ultimatum & Recto's Counter-Offensive:**
  - Catholic authorities threatened to shut down all Catholic universities and schools across the country if the bill passed into law.
  - Recto boldly countered that if the Church closed its schools, the government would immediately move to **nationalize all educational institutions**, which would benefit Philippine sovereignty. He quipped: *"They [the Catholic Church] are making too much profit which they can ill-afford to give up."*
  - Recto clarified: *"Rizal did not pretend to teach religion or theology when he wrote these books. He aimed at inculcating civic consciousness in the Filipinos, national dignity, personal pride, and PATRIOTISM..."*

### 3. Senator Jose P. Laurel's Compromise & Enactment
- To break the bitter two-week congressional deadlock, Sen. Jose P. Laurel formulated a substitute/compromise version:
  - **Removal of "Compulsion":** Removed the explicit term "compulsory" in general context.
  - **Religious Exemption Clause:** Allowed students with genuine religious objections to request exemption from reading the unexpurgated novels by submitting a **sworn written statement** (*affidavit*).
  - ⚠️ **EXAM TRAP:** The exemption excuses the student **ONLY from reading the unexpurgated novels**, but **NEVER exempts them from taking the Rizal course itself!**
- **Voting and Approval:**
  - **Senate:** Approved unanimously (23 senators in favor) on SB 438.
  - **House of Representatives:** Approved HB 5564 with **71 in favor**, **9 against**, and **2 abstentions**.
  - **Signing:** Signed into law by President **Ramon Magsaysay** on **June 12, 1956** as **Republic Act No. 1425** (The Rizal Law).

### 4. Anatomy of Republic Act No. 1425 (Key Sections)
| Section | Mandate / Legal Provision |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Section 1** | Mandates courses on the life, works, and writings of Jose Rizal, particularly *Noli Me Tangere* and *El Filibusterismo*, in the curricula of all public and private schools, colleges, and universities. Mandates unexpurgated editions or English translations in collegiate courses. Contains the religious belief sworn statement exemption clause. |
| **Section 2** | Obligates all educational institutions to keep in their libraries an adequate number of unexpurgated copies of the novels and other writings of Rizal. The Board of National Education determines adequacy based on school enrollment. |
| **Section 3** | Directs the Board of National Education to cause the translation of *Noli* and *Fili* into English, Tagalog, and major Philippine dialects; print cheap, popular editions; and distribute them **free of charge** via *Purok* organizations and Barrio Councils. |
| **Section 4** | Clarifies that nothing in the Act repeals or amends **Section 927 of the Administrative Code** (prohibiting public school teachers from discussing religious doctrines). |
| **Section 5** | Authorizes the appropriation of **₱300,000** out of the National Treasury to carry out the implementation of the Act. |
| **Section 6** | Effectivity clause: The Act takes effect immediately upon its approval (June 12, 1956). |

### 5. Reinforcements in the 1990s
- **Memorandum Order No. 246 (Dec 26, 1994):** Issued by President **Fidel V. Ramos**, directing the Department of Education, Culture and Sports (DECS) and CHED to strictly enforce RA 1425 in all educational institutions.
- **CHED Memo No. 3 (1995) & CHED Memo No. 6 (1996):** Mandated the enforcement of RA 1425 as a prerequisite 3-unit collegiate course for graduation across all degree programs.

### 6. The Selection of the National Hero: The Taft Commission (1901)
- In 1901, during the American colonial administration, Civil Governor **William Howard Taft** convened a commission to establish an official national hero who would serve as an inspiring, unifying civic model.
- **Taft Commission Members:**
  - *American Members:* William Howard Taft (Chair), Morgan Shuster, Bernard Moses, Dean Worcester, Henry Clay Ide.
  - *Filipino Members:* Trinidad Pardo H. de Tavera, Gregorio Araneta, Cayetano Arellano, Jose Luzurriaga.
- **4 Strict Criteria for Selection:**
  1. **Must be a Filipino** (*Isang Pilipino*).
  2. **Must be deceased / dead** (*Yumao na*) — to avoid potential subsequent controversies or political complications.
  3. **Must have possessed towering / uncompromising love for country** (*May matayog na pagmamahal sa bayan*).
  4. **Must have possessed a calm, peaceful, and restrained temperament** (*May mahinahong damdamin*).
- **Candidates Evaluated:**
  1. **Graciano Lopez-Jaena (1856–1896):** Jaro, Iloilo; orator and founder/first editor of *La Solidaridad*; died of TB in Barcelona. Disqualified due to radical lifestyle and temper.
  2. **Marcelo H. del Pilar (1850–1896):** Cupang, Bulacan; prolific satirist (*Dasalan at Tocsohan*), editor of *La Solidaridad*; died of TB in Barcelona. Held militant anti-friar and revolutionary leanings.
  3. **Antonio Luna (1866–1899):** Urbiztondo, Binondo; pharmacist, general in the Phil-American War; assassinated in Cabanatuan. Too hot-tempered, military-oriented.
  4. **Emilio Jacinto (1875–1899):** Trozo, Tondo; *"Brain of the Katipunan"* (*Utak ng Katipunan*), author of the *Kartilya*; died of malaria in Laguna. Purely armed revolutionary figure.
  5. **Dr. José Rizal (1861–1896):** Selected as the National Hero.
- **Why Rizal was Chosen:**
  1. *Unifying Awakening:* He was the very first Filipino whose writings awakened the entire archipelago into a cohesive national consciousness that sparked the 1896 revolution.
  2. *Paragon of Peace:* Advocated peaceful, gradual reforms through education and civic virtue rather than armed bloodshed, fitting American desires for colonial peace.
  3. *Appealed to Filipino Sentimentality:* His tragic martyrdom at Bagumbayan touched the deeply sentimental soul of the Filipino nation.

## Checkpoint 2: The 19th Century Global & Philippine Context (The Three Revolutions & Colonial Evils)

### 1. Global Context: The Three Great Revolutions
The 19th century was defined by global economic and political upheavals that reshaped the world and directly catalyzed Philippine nationalism:
1. **The Industrial Revolution (England & Europe):**
   - Transformation from agrarian feudalism to **industrial capitalism**.
   - Inventions: The steam engine, mechanized textile factories, railways, steamships.
   - Social impacts: Mass migration from rural farms to factory cities; creation of the industrial working class and the bourgeoisie (commercial middle class).
   - Speed of trade and communication accelerated exponentially, reducing geographical barriers.
2. **The French Revolution (1789–1799):**
   - Shattered absolute divine-right monarchies in Europe; established democratic and constitutional governance.
   - Core egalitarian motto: **Freedom, Fairness, and Fraternity** (*Liberté, Égalité, Fraternité*).
   - Ideals crossed the Pyrenees into Spain, instigating repeated clashes between Spanish liberals and royalist conservatives throughout the 19th century.
3. **The American Revolution (1776):**
   - The first modern colonial revolt to overthrow European imperial dominance; gave birth to a democratic republic founded on self-governance.
   - Introduced philosophies of constitutional government, human rights, and the separation of powers (Montesquieu, Jean-Jacques Rousseau, John Locke, Thomas Paine, Thomas Jefferson).

### 2. The Age of Enlightenment & Rise of Social Sciences
- Emergence of Sociology, Anthropology, and modern historiography that prioritized **human reason, empirical observation, and scientific inquiry** over blind church dogma.
- Philosophers like **Michel de Montaigne** demonstrated that reason is the ultimate weapon against ignorance, royal autocracy, and inherited class aristocracy.
- **Anti-Clericalism in Europe & Spain:** Spanish liberals viewed the excessive power and wealth of the Catholic Church as the greatest barrier to political modernization, sparking campaigns to strip religious orders of political and educational monopolies.

### 3. The Opening of the Suez Canal (November 17, 1869)
- **Vital Engineering Feat:** 103 miles long, cutting across the Isthmus of Suez in Egypt; designed and constructed by French diplomat-engineer **Ferdinand de Lesseps**.
- Connected the Mediterranean Sea directly with the Red Sea and the Indian Ocean.
- **Revolutionary Travel Impact:**
  - Before 1869: Steamers sailing from Barcelona/Europe to Manila had to voyage around the dangerous **Cape of Good Hope** (Africa), taking **more than 3 months** (over 90–120 days).
  - After 1869: Journey was slashed down to **only 32 days**!
- **Socio-Political Impact on the Philippines:**
  - Brought the Philippines closer to Spain and Europe.
  - Rapid inflow of foreign trade, books, newspapers, and liberal ideas (Rousseau's *Social Contract*, Locke's *Two Treatises of Government*, Paine's *Common Sense*).
  - Enabled affluent Filipino families to send their sons (*Ilustrados*) to study in the universities of Europe.

### 4. Democratic Rule of Gov. Gen. Carlos María de la Torre (1869–1871)
- Following the liberal revolution of 1868 in Spain, **Carlos María de la Torre** was dispatched as Governor-General.
- **Hallmarks of Administration:**
  - Avoided ostentatious luxury; wore ordinary civilian clothes and walked without armed bodyguards.
  - **Abolished press censorship** and recognized freedom of speech and assembly.
  - Gave Filipinos a transformative **foretaste of democratic rule** and human dignity.
  - Tolerated and encouraged the Filipino secularization movement led by **Father José Burgos**, who agitated to replace Spanish regular friars with native secular clerics as parish priests.

### 5. The Cavite Mutiny (January 20, 1872) & Martyrdom of GOMBURZA
- After De la Torre was replaced by the tyrannical reactionary **Gov. Gen. Rafael de Izquierdo**, Izquierdo abruptly abolished the traditional exemptions of Filipino shipyard workers and artillery soldiers at Cavite from paying tribute and rendering forced labor (*polo y servicio*).
- On **January 20, 1872**, Sergeant Lamadrid led approximately 200 Filipino soldiers and arsenal workers in an uprising at Fort San Felipe, Cavite.
- Spanish authorities used the mutiny as a convenient pretext to liquidate the outspoken leaders of the secularization movement: **Father Mariano Gomez**, **Father José Burgos**, and **Father Jacinto Zamora** (**GOMBURZA**).
- **Execution:** Sentenced to death on trumped-up charges of sedition and executed by **garrote** in Bagumbayan on **February 17, 1872**.
- **Impact on Rizal:**
  - **Paciano Rizal** was a housemate, student, and close associate of Father Burgos; he witnessed the horror and was blacklisted.
  - Paciano inspired his younger brother José (then 11 years old) to take up the patriotic crusade.
  - Rizal later dedicated his second novel, ***El Filibusterismo* (1891)**, to the sacred memory of GOMBURZA.

### 6. The 11 Evils of Spanish Colonial Rule in the 19th Century
| # | Colonial Evil | Historical Manifestation in the Philippines |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | **Instability of Colonial Administration** | Frequent shifts between liberal and conservative governments in Madrid caused chaotic turnover in governors-general (dozens in a single decade), preventing stable governance. |
| **2** | **Corrupt Colonial Officials** | Ignorant, ruthless, and bankrupt Spanish officials were sent to colonies to enrich themselves through embezzlement, bribery, and extortion. |
| **3** | **Denial of Representation in Spanish Cortes** | While Spain enjoyed constitutional representation, Philippine representation in the Spanish parliament was revoked in 1837 and never restored. |
| **4** | **Human Rights Denied to Filipinos** | Freedom of speech, assembly, religion, and press granted to Spanish citizens were strictly denied to brown Filipinos. |
| **5** | **No Equality Before the Law** | The Spanish legal code favored whites; laws were applied stringently to natives while Spaniards operated with complete impunity. |
| **6** | **Maladministration of Justice** | Courts were notoriously venal, corrupt, and glacial. Lawsuits dragged on for decades, impoverishing native litigants. |
| **7** | **Racial Discrimination** | Caucasians treated native Filipinos as an inferior race, branding them derisively as ***Indios*** across the civil service, military, universities, and society. |
| **8** | **Frailocracy (*Frailocracia*)** | Friars controlled municipal government, schools, police reports, and tax rolls, wielding more practical authority than the Governor-General himself. |
| **9** | **Forced Labor (*Polo y servicio*)** | Compulsory labor of 40 days per year required of male Filipinos (16–60 years old) for public infrastructure without pay, often resulting in injury and starvation. |
| **10** | **Friar Haciendas** | Religious orders accumulated immense agrarian estates, impoverishing native tenant farmers (*inquilinos*) through arbitrary land rents (e.g., Calamba hacienda crisis). |
| **11** | **The Guardia Civil** | Paramilitary police corps established to suppress bandits, but notorious for harassing, torturing, and murdering innocent Filipino civilians with zero oversight. |

## Checkpoint 3: Rizal's Secret Mission, Early Travels Abroad & Madrid Academic Life (1882–1885)

### 1. The Secret Mission & Departure
- **The Core Motivation:** Rizal's disillusionment with the Dominican professors at the University of Santo Tomas (UST) who discriminated against native students, coupled with his burning mission to prepare himself to liberate his people from Spanish tyranny.
- **The Secret Mission:** To keen observe the life, culture, languages, industries, governments, and laws of European nations to prepare himself for the task of freeing his oppressed motherland.
- **Secrecy:** Kept strictly secret from Spanish authorities and even from his own parents (Don Francisco and Doña Teodora) to avoid blocking his travel. Only his brother **Paciano**, uncle Antonio Rivera, and sisters Saturnina and Lucia were in the know.
- **Departure Date:** **May 3, 1882**.
- **Vessel & Passport:** Boarded the Spanish steamer **SS Salvadora** bound for Singapore, traveling under the alias passport of **José Mercado** (provided by his cousin/uncle to bypass colonial surveillance).

### 2. Voyage from Manila to Barcelona: Chronological Route
```mermaid
flowchart LR
    A[May 3, 1882: Manila - SS Salvadora] --> B[May 9, 1882: Singapore - Hotel de la Paz]
    B --> C[May 11: Boarded Djemnah]
    C --> D[May 17: Punta de Gales, Ceylon]
    D --> E[May 18: Colombo]
    E --> F[May 27: Aden - First Camels]
    F --> G[June 2: Suez Canal & Port Said]
    G --> H[June 11: Naples - First European Soil]
    H --> I[June 12: Marseilles - Chateau d'If]
    I --> J[June 15: Port Bou - Pyrenees Train]
    J --> K[June 16, 1882: Arrived Barcelona]
```

- **May 9, 1882 — Singapore:** SS Salvadora docked. Rizal stayed at the **Hotel de la Paz** for two days.
- **May 11, 1882 — Boarding the Djemnah:** Transferred to the **Djemnah**, a French passenger steamer bound for Europe. Rizal practiced conversational French with fellow international passengers.
- **May 17, 1882 — Punta de Gales (Point Galle, Ceylon):** Rizal noted in his diary: *"The general appearance of Point Galle is picturesque but lonely and quiet and at the same time sad."*
- **May 18, 1882 — Colombo (Ceylon):** Arrived Colombo; admired its elegant buildings and lush vegetation.
- **May 27, 1882 — Aden (Yemen):** Landed at 8:30 AM. Rizal observed that Aden was **"a city hotter than Manila"** and was amused to see real camels for the first time in his life.
- **June 2, 1882 — City of Suez & The Suez Canal:** Arrived at the terminal of the Suez Canal. Rizal admired the moonlight over the Red Sea, which filled him with nostalgia for Calamba and his family. Traversed the 103-mile canal to **Port Said** (Mediterranean terminal).
- **June 11, 1882 — Naples, Italy:** Disembarked and spent one hour exploring Naples with a guide. **This was the very first European soil José Rizal ever set foot upon.** He praised its business vitality, lively Italian populace, and panoramic landscape.
- **June 12, 1882 — Marseilles, France:** The Djemnah docked at 10:00 PM. Rizal slept on board, then stayed 2.5 days in Marseilles. He visited the famous **Château d'If**, the island fortress prison where Edmond Dantès, hero of Alexandre Dumas' *The Count of Monte Cristo*, was imprisoned.
- **June 15, 1882 — Across the Pyrenees:** Left Marseilles by express train, crossing the Pyrenees border and stopping for an inspection day at the frontier town of **Port Bou**.
- **June 16, 1882 (12:00 Noon) — Arrival in Barcelona:** Checked into **Fonda de España** on Calle San Pablo. His initial impression of Barcelona was unfavorable (found it dingy and residents discourteous), but he soon grew to appreciate its atmosphere of freedom and liberalism.

### 3. Life and Literary Awakening in Barcelona (1882)
- ***Amor Patrio* (Love of Country):**
  - First nationalistic essay written on foreign soil.
  - Penned under the pen-name **Laong Laan** (*Ever Prepared*).
  - Sent to Manila and published on **August 20, 1882** in the bilingual newspaper ***Diarong Tagalog*** (founded by Basilio Teodoro Moran).
  - Published in two parallel texts: the original Spanish text by Rizal, and a Tagalog translation penned by **Marcelo H. del Pilar**.
- **Other Barcelona Essays:**
  - ***Los Viajes* (Travels):** Celebrated the educational value of travel.
  - ***Revista de Madrid* (Review of Madrid):** Returned unpublished because *Diarong Tagalog* ceased operations due to financial difficulties.
- **Move to Madrid:** Following Paciano's advice, Rizal relocated to Madrid in the fall of 1882 to continue his university education in the Spanish capital.

### 4. University Studies at Universidad Central de Madrid (1882–1885)
- **November 3, 1882:** Rizal formally enrolled at the prestigious **Universidad Central de Madrid** in two concurrent degree programs:
  1. **Medicine**
  2. **Philosophy and Letters**
- **Academy of Fine Arts of San Fernando:** Studied painting and sculpture.
- **Extracurricular Rigor:**
  - Took lessons in French, German, and English under private language tutors.
  - Practiced fencing and pistol shooting at the **Hall of Arms of Sanz y Carbonell**.
  - Read extensively, economizing on food and basic clothing to purchase second-hand classical and medical books.
- **Círculo Hispano-Filipino:**
  - An association formed by Filipino students and liberal Spaniards in Madrid.
  - Upon request of its members, Rizal penned the nostalgic poem ***Me Piden Versos* (They Ask Me For Verses)**, which he personally declaimed at the New Year's Eve banquet on **December 31, 1882**.
- **Joining Freemasonry:**
  - **March 1883:** Joined the Masonic Lodge **Acacia** in Madrid. His core reason: to secure the international support of Freemasonry in the Filipino struggle against friar tyranny and Spanish oppression.
  - **November 1883:** Transferred to **Lodge Solidaridad No. 53**, where he later attained the rank of **Master Mason**.
- **Conferment of Academic Degrees:**
  - **June 1884:** Conferred the degree of **Licentiate in Medicine** (*Licenciado en Medicina*).
  - **1884–1885:** Passed all subjects for the Doctor of Medicine (*Doctor en Medicina*). However, he was only formally awarded the Doctor's diploma in **1887** because he could not afford to pay the hefty graduation and diploma fees (*derechos de grado*).
  - **June 1885:** Conferred the degree of **Licentiate in Philosophy and Letters** (*Licenciado en Filosofia y Letras*) by Universidad Central de Madrid with the highest rating of **"Sobresaliente" (Excellent)**.

## Checkpoint 4: Ophthalmology Specialization, Publication of Noli Me Tangere & The Grand European Tour (1885–1887)

### 1. Paris to Berlin: Ophthalmology Specialization (1885–1886)
- **The Calling:** Rizal decided to specialize in **ophthalmology** (eye medicine and surgery) primarily to **cure his mother's (Doña Teodora) failing eyesight / cataract**.
- **Paris (October 1885 – February 1886):**
  - Worked as a clinical assistant to **Dr. Louis de Weckert** (1832–1904), the leading French ophthalmologist of the era.
  - Rapidly mastered surgical techniques for eye operations.
  - Visited fellow expatriates: Trinidad, Felix, and Paz Pardo de Tavera, and painter **Juan Luna**.
  - **Posed as Model for Juan Luna's Famous Canvases:**
    - In ***The Blood Compact* (El Pacto de Sangre)**: Rizal posed as the Boholano chieftain **Sikatuna**, while Trinidad Pardo de Tavera posed as the conquistador **Miguel López de Legazpi**.
    - In ***The Death of Cleopatra***: Rizal dressed in Egyptian costume and posed as an ancient Egyptian priest/scribe.
- **Heidelberg, Germany (Arrived February 3, 1886):**
  - Left Paris on February 1 and arrived in historic Heidelberg on Feb 3, 1886.
  - Worked at the **University Eye Hospital** under the renowned German ophthalmologist **Dr. Otto Becker**.
  - Attended lectures by Dr. Becker and Prof. Wilhelm Kuehne.
  - Wrote his celebrated poem ***A Las Flores de Heidelberg* (To the Flowers of Heidelberg)** on April 22, 1886.
- **Letter to Trinidad Rizal (March 11, 1886):**
  - Rizal wrote an important letter to his sister Trinidad expressing unbounded admiration for **German womanhood**.
  - Rizal characterized German women as: **serious, diligent, educated, friendly, hardworking, and devoid of frivolous gossip or quarrelsome vanity**. He urged Filipino women to emulate their dignity and intellectual depth.

### 2. Berlin: Intellectual Nexus & Historic Publication (November 1886 – May 1887)
- Arrived in Berlin in **November 1886**. Enchanted by the absence of racial prejudice and the vibrant scientific atmosphere.
- **Inducted into German Scientific Circles:**
  - **Dr. Feodor Jagor:** Celebrated German scientist-traveler and author of *Travels in the Philippines* (*Reisen auf den Philippinen*), a book Rizal had read and admired as a student in Manila.
  - **Dr. Rudolf Virchow:** World-renowned German pathologist, anthropologist, and founder of cellular pathology.
  - **Dr. Hans Virchow:** Professor of anatomy (son of Rudolf Virchow).
  - **Dr. W. Joest:** Eminent German geographer.
  - Rizal was inducted as an active member into the **Anthropological Society**, **Ethnological Society**, and **Geographical Society of Berlin** (the first Asian to receive such honors).
- **5 Prime Reasons for Rizal's Sojourn in Berlin:**
  1. To gain advanced clinical knowledge in Ophthalmology.
  2. To further his studies of science and European languages.
  3. To observe the economic and political conditions of the German nation.
  4. To associate with renowned German scientists, scholars, and anthropologists.
  5. **To complete and publish his masterpiece novel, *Noli Me Tangere*.**

### 3. The Bleak Winter of 1886 & The Printing of *Noli Me Tangere*
- **The Darkest Winter:** The bleak winter of 1886 was the darkest chapter in Rizal's life:
  - No remittances arrived from Calamba (due to locust plagues and collapsing sugar prices).
  - Rizal was penniless, hungry, sick, and forced to eat only one meal of bread and water per day.
  - He pawned the **diamond ring** given to him by his eldest sister, **Saturnina**, to pay rent and avoid eviction.
- **Literary Inspiration:**
  - Rizal was inspired to write a novel exposing colonial agony after reading Harriet Beecher Stowe's ***Uncle Tom's Cabin*** (which depicted the horrific plight of enslaved African-Americans in the United States).
- **The Savior of the *Noli* — Dr. Máximo Viola:**
  - A wealthy doctor friend from San Miguel, Bulacan, arrived in Berlin at the height of Rizal's despondency.
  - Upon learning of Rizal's starvation and the completed novel, Viola gladly financed Rizal's living expenses and loaned him the funds needed to print the book.
- **Printing Milestone:**
  - Manuscript completed and ready for press: **February 21, 1887**.
  - Printing Shop: ***Berliner Buchdruckerei-Actien-Gesellschaft*** (charged the lowest fee: **₱300 for 2,000 copies**).
  - **March 21, 1887:** *Noli Me Tangere* officially came off the press! Rizal immediately sent the first copies to Ferdinand Blumentritt, Dr. Antonio Ma. Regidor, and Graciano Lopez-Jaena, and gave the original manuscript and printer's pen to Máximo Viola with heartfelt gratitude.
- **The Title:** Latin for ***"Touch Me Not"***, taken from the Gospel of St. John (Chapter 20, Verses 13–17), spoken by the resurrected Christ to Mary Magdalene.

### 4. The Grand European Tour with Dr. Máximo Viola (May–June 1887)
With remaining funds loaned by Viola, Rizal embarked on an educational grand tour across Central and Southern Europe:
- **May 11, 1887 — Leaving Berlin:** Rizal and Viola departed Berlin by train.
- **Dresden, Germany:** Visited the regional art museum; deeply impressed by the masterpiece painting ***Prometheus Bound***.
- **Teschen (now Děčín, Czechia):** Stopover after leaving Dresden.
- **Leitmeritz, Bohemia (May 13–16, 1887):**
  - At 1:30 PM, May 13, arrived at Leitmeritz station.
  - **First in-person meeting** between Rizal and his lifelong intellectual correspondent, **Professor Ferdinand Blumentritt** (kind-hearted Austrian professor and ethnologist).
  - Stayed at Hotel Krebs. Met the **Burgomaster** (town mayor).
  - Spoke extemporaneously in fluent German at the **Tourist's Club of Leitmeritz** (where Blumentritt served as secretary).
  - Met renowned scientist **Dr. Carlos Czepelak** and eminent naturalist **Professor Robert Klutschak**.
  - Left Leitmeritz on May 16 at 9:45 AM amid emotional farewells.
- **Prague (Czechia):** Carried letters of introduction to **Dr. Willkomm** (professor of natural history at University of Prague). Viola famously noted: *"Nothing of importance happened in this city."*
- **Vienna, Austria (May 20, 1887):**
  - Arrived in Vienna, capital of the Austro-Hungarian Empire, hailed as the **"Queen of the Danube"** for its imperial architecture, waltzes, and artistic charm.
  - Met **Norfentals**, one of Austria's greatest novelists, who became enthralled by Rizal's genius.
  - Stayed at **Hotel Metropole**, where Rizal successfully recovered his **lost diamond stickpin** returned by the hotel maid.
- **Danubian River Voyage to Linz (May 24, 1887):**
  - Traveled by river boat along the Danube to Linz.
  - **Paper Napkins Curiosity:** Rizal noted with great interest that passengers used **paper napkins** during meals. Viola remarked that paper napkins were *"more hygienic and economical than cloth napkins."*
- **Munich, Nuremberg & Ulm (Germany):**
  - Munich: Sampled famous **Munich beer** (reputed the finest in Germany).
  - Nuremberg: Explored one of Germany's oldest preserved medieval walled cities; inspected torture instruments from the Inquisition.
  - Ulm: Visited the **Cathedral of Ulm**, the largest and tallest cathedral spire in all of Germany.
  - Stuttgart & Baden: Explored city architecture and mineral baths.
  - **Rheinfall (Cascade of the Rhine):** Marveled at Rheinfall, universally acclaimed as *"the most beautiful waterfall of Europe."*
- **Crossing the Frontier to Switzerland (June 2–3, 1887):**
  - Stayed at **Schaffhausen**, Basel, Bern, and Lausanne.
- **Geneva, Switzerland (June 1887):**
  - Spent 15 delightful days in Geneva, one of Europe's most pristine cities.
  - **June 19, 1887 — 26th Birthday:** Rizal treated Viola to a festive blow-out lunch to celebrate his 26th birthday.
  - **Outrage over the Madrid Exposition:** Received news of the **Exposition of the Philippines in Madrid**, where native **Igorots of Northern Luzon** were displayed like zoo animals in primitive loincloths, contracting pneumonia in cold Spanish weather. Rizal was deeply insulted by this racial degradation of his countrymen.
  - **June 23, 1887:** Rizal and Viola parted ways—Viola returned to Barcelona, while Rizal proceeded to Italy.
- **Rizal in Italy (June 1887):**
  - **June 27, 1887 — Rome:** Reached Rome, the *"Eternal City"* and *"City of the Caesars."* Thrilled by the grandeur of the Roman Forum and Colosseum, he wrote to Blumentritt: *"I am tired as a dog, but I will sleep as a God."*
  - **June 29, 1887 — The Vatican:** On the **Feast Day of St. Peter and St. Paul**, Rizal entered Vatican City, the *"City of the Popes"* and capital of Christendom.
- **Homeward Bound:** After a week in Rome, Rizal packed his luggage and wrote to his father Francisco that he was finally returning home to Calamba.

## Master Summary & High-Yield Exam Traps Cheat Sheet

| Topic / Question | Correct Fact | Common Distractor / Exam Trap |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Original Author of Rizal Bill** | **Senator Claro M. Recto** | *Sen. Jose P. Laurel* (Laurel was the Committee Chairman and Sponsor, NOT the original author). |
| **Religious Exemption under RA 1425** | Excuses student **ONLY from reading unexpurgated novels** upon submitting a sworn written statement. | It does **NOT** exempt the student from enrolling in or attending the Rizal course! |
| **Unexpurgated Editions Requirement** | Obligatory in **Collegiate courses** (Higher Education). | High school curricula use expurgated/simplified editions, not unexpurgated. |
| **Appropriation Amount for RA 1425** | **₱300,000** from the National Treasury (Section 5). | Do not confuse with the ₱300 printing cost of the *Noli*! |
| **Opposing Fr. Cavanna's Ratio** | **25 patriotic pages vs 120 anti-Catholic pages** out of 333 pages. | Common exam trap: Inverting the numbers (e.g., claiming 120 patriotic pages). |
| **National Hero Selection Board** | **1901 Taft Commission** under William Howard Taft. | Not chosen by the Malolos Congress or Katipunan; selected under American civil auspices. |
| **Taft Commission Criteria** | 1) Filipino, 2) Dead, 3) Towering patriotism, 4) Calm/peaceful temperament. | Living persons were strictly disqualified. |
| **Rizal's Travel Alias** | **José Mercado** (on passport). | *Laong Laan* was his pen name; *Dimasalang* was his Masonic name. |
| **First European Soil Visited** | **Naples, Italy** (June 11, 1882 - 1 hour tour). | *Marseilles* or *Barcelona* are frequent incorrect guesses. |
| **First Article Abroad** | ***Amor Patrio*** (Love of Country). | Written in Barcelona, published in *Diarong Tagalog* (Aug 20, 1882); translated to Tagalog by M.H. del Pilar. |
| **Savior of *Noli Me Tangere*** | **Dr. Máximo Viola** (from San Miguel, Bulacan). | *Valentin Ventura* was the savior of *El Filibusterismo* (1891), NOT *Noli*! |
| **Meaning of *Noli Me Tangere*** | Latin for **"Touch Me Not"** (Gospel of St. John 20:13–17). | Not "Do Not Touch My Country" or "The Social Cancer" (which was Charles Derbyshire's English title). |
| **Printer of *Noli Me Tangere*** | *Berliner Buchdruckerei-Actien-Gesellschaft* (**₱300 for 2,000 copies**). | Finished Feb 21, 1887; off the press March 21, 1887. |
| **First In-Person Meeting with Blumentritt** | **Leitmeritz, Bohemia** (May 13, 1887, at 1:30 PM). | They had corresponded in letters for years before meeting in Leitmeritz. |
| **Why Ophthalmology?** | To cure his **mother's (Doña Teodora) cataract/failing eyes**. | Not for financial wealth or colonial Prestige. |
| **Mentors in Ophthalmology** | **Dr. Louis de Weckert** (Paris) and **Dr. Otto Becker** (Heidelberg). | *Dr. Rudolf Virchow* was an anthropologist/pathologist, not his ophthalmology instructor. |
| **Suez Canal Length & Architect** | **103 miles long**, built by **Ferdinand de Lesseps**, opened **Nov 17, 1869**. | Reduced travel from 3+ months to **32 days**. |
| **Governor-General of Liberty** | **Carlos María de la Torre** (1869–1871; simple life, abolished censorship). | *Rafael de Izquierdo* was the tyrant who executed GOMBURZA. |
| **Dedication of Rizal's Novels** | *Noli Me Tangere* is dedicated to the **Fatherland (Inang Bayan)**; *El Filibusterismo* is dedicated to **GOMBURZA**. | Inverting these two dedications is a classic collegiate board exam trap! |
